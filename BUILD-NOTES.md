Build notes for the Famous Last Words at 1 Percent entry.

The entry is one line of 85 characters, written by main.py to the output entry file. The line reads: I stayed at 1% for an hour on purpose, just to watch you panic. Best hour of my life.

Why this line. The judge wants the phone itself talking, in its own voice, about its own dying battery, with a real twist and a sharp final beat. This line gives the phone agency: it did not fade helplessly, it chose to linger at 1% for an hour to watch its owner panic, and calls that the best hour of its life. The twist is that the dying phone is the one enjoying itself. The punch lands at the end, on the words Best hour of my life.

What was deliberately avoided. No will reading, no farewell to the charger, and no pun on 1 percent, since the judge names those as stock moves. The line also never explains itself and never steps out of the phone's voice into a narrator.

Verification. The program was run and the output file was checked. The file holds exactly the line above, 85 characters and 85 bytes, with no trailing newline, non-blank, and inside the gate of 1 to 199 characters. The SHA-256 of the file as written is dac1490570d2e4f1bf61506831bbf845b1b27277c3744b5063fa9a6cf3124428. With a trailing newline added the hash would be 26da3120c3cfbe5f1838578a418e8798e272d78326cbae891e59bced79a45410.

The program also guards the gate itself: it refuses to write anything shorter than 1 character, longer than 199 characters, or blank. There is no floor or benchmark for this bounty, so no local benchmark run applies. The entry needs no network, no secrets, and no keypair.
